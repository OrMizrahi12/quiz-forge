Question 1
A support team sends 2,000 short order-status replies a day, and each one must be ready in under three seconds. They are choosing a model tier for the drafts. Where should they start?
A) Opus, because the most capable tier makes the fewest mistakes
B) Sonnet, because the recommended default fits every job
C) Haiku, then test it on a sample of real replies
D) The biggest tier first, stepping down only if usage runs out
Answer: C
Quote: "Start with the smallest tier that can clearly do the job."
Why: High volume and tight speed needs pull toward the smallest tier, tested on the real task.

Question 1
A researcher will run one analysis of a messy clinical dataset, once, and a wrong conclusion would be expensive to undo. Time and usage are not a concern this week. Which choice fits best?
A) The most capable tier, accepting a slower and costlier reply
B) The fastest tier, because a single job doesn't need more
C) The fastest tier, run five times, keeping the most common answer
D) Whichever tier is selected by default in the app
Answer: A
Quote: "A one-off job where accuracy is everything pulls the other way."
Why: One job where being right matters most pulls away from the small, fast tier.
