# Kneading and gluten

## What kneading does

When flour meets water, two proteins join into gluten, a stretchy network that traps the yeast's gas. Kneading lines up that network so the dough can stretch without tearing.

## The windowpane test

Stretch a small piece of dough between your fingers until light shows through it. If it tears before it turns thin and see-through, knead for a few more minutes and test again.

## Resting instead of kneading

A dough left to rest for 20 to 30 minutes after mixing builds gluten on its own, so it needs less kneading afterwards. This rest is called autolyse when the salt and yeast are added after it. Wet doughs are often folded every 30 minutes instead of kneaded, which builds the same strength with less work.
